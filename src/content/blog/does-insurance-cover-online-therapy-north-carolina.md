Many people searching for a therapist in North Carolina want the same clarity: **will my insurance help pay for online therapy?** The short answer is: sometimes yes—but it depends on your plan, the clinician, the service, and whether the session meets the insurer’s rules.

This article explains how coverage for telehealth therapy typically works, what questions to ask, and how TrueBridge approaches insurance and cost conversations. It is educational information only. It is not a guarantee of coverage, benefits, or reimbursement for any specific plan.

## The short version

Insurance **can** cover online therapy in North Carolina when:

- Your clinician participates with your plan (is “in network”) or your plan allows out-of-network benefits you choose to use
- The service is a covered mental health benefit (for example, individual psychotherapy)
- The session is considered medically necessary under your plan’s rules
- Telehealth is allowed for that service under your plan (most modern plans do allow it, but details vary)

Insurance **often does not** neatly cover:

- Couples or relationship counseling billed as its own service
- Sessions with a clinician who is not credentialed with your plan
- Services your plan excludes or limits

When coverage does not apply, private-pay is usually the alternative. TrueBridge publishes standard private-pay session rates on [Insurance & Rates](/insurance-rates/).

## Why “online” is usually not the hard part anymore

During and after the pandemic, many insurers expanded telehealth benefits. For a lot of commercial plans, a covered therapy session delivered by video is treated similarly to an in-office visit—**if** the clinician and service would have been covered in person.

So the bigger questions are usually:

1. Does this clinician participate with my insurance?
2. Is this type of therapy a covered benefit?
3. What will I owe (copay, coinsurance, deductible)?

## What “accepts my insurance” actually means

People often search for a “therapist that accepts [insurance] in NC.” That usually means the clinician is **in network** with that payer: the practice has a contract, billing uses the plan’s rates, and your cost-sharing follows your plan.

Important nuances:

- **Participation can vary by clinician**, even inside the same practice.
- **Credentialing takes time.** A practice may be preparing to join networks before any clinician is officially live with a given insurer.
- **Being in network does not erase your deductible, copay, or visit limits.**

## Couples therapy and insurance

Partners frequently ask whether insurance covers couples therapy. In many plans, relationship counseling is **not** covered as its own service. Coverage, when it exists at all, may only apply when sessions are billed under one partner’s mental health diagnosis and meet medical-necessity criteria—and some plans exclude relationship counseling entirely.

Because of that, many couples choose private-pay for clarity. TrueBridge explains options before you begin. Learn more on our [couples therapy](/services/couples-therapy/) page and [Insurance & Rates](/insurance-rates/).

## How to verify benefits before you start

A useful benefits check usually answers:

- Is mental health outpatient therapy a covered benefit?
- Is telehealth allowed for therapy visits?
- What are my deductible, copay, and coinsurance?
- Are there session limits or prior authorization requirements?
- Is this specific clinician (or practice tax ID / NPI) in network?

You can call the member services number on your insurance card. A therapy practice can often help interpret what they find—but **your insurer’s explanation of benefits is the final authority**, and estimates can change.

At TrueBridge, our goal is simple: help you understand likely out-of-pocket cost **before** ongoing care begins so money is not a surprise mid-process.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does North Carolina law require insurers to cover telehealth therapy?

Coverage rules depend on your specific plan type (commercial, marketplace, Medicaid, Medicare Advantage, etc.) and the insurer’s policies. Do not assume every plan treats every telehealth therapy visit the same way—verify for your card and plan year.

### Will online therapy cost the same as in-person therapy under insurance?

Often cost-sharing is similar when both formats are covered, but your plan controls the details. Confirm with your insurer.

### Can I use out-of-network benefits?

Some plans allow it; many high-deductible or HMO-style plans make it limited or impractical. Ask your insurer about out-of-network mental health benefits and any paperwork required.
