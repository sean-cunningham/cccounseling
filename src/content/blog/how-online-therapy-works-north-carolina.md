Online therapy (also called telehealth or virtual therapy) lets you meet with a licensed clinician by video from home or another private space. For many adults in North Carolina, it is a practical way to get consistent care without commuting—especially when schedules, childcare, or distance would otherwise get in the way.

This guide explains how online therapy typically works in North Carolina, what to expect in a session, and how TrueBridge approaches virtual care. It is general information, not legal or clinical advice for your specific situation.

## Who can provide online therapy in North Carolina?

In general, therapy that is delivered to a client located in North Carolina should be provided by a clinician who is appropriately licensed to practice in North Carolina (or otherwise authorized under applicable telehealth rules). Licensing boards set the rules for each profession (for example, counseling, marriage and family therapy, psychology, or social work).

When you are choosing a practice, it is reasonable to ask:

- Is the clinician licensed in North Carolina?
- Will sessions be held while you are physically in North Carolina?
- What platform do they use for secure video sessions?

TrueBridge provides therapy for adults across North Carolina primarily through telehealth, with licensed clinicians matched to your needs when possible. Depending on scheduling and clinician availability, limited in-person options may sometimes be available in the Cary and Raleigh area.

## What an online therapy session usually looks like

Most virtual sessions follow a familiar therapy rhythm:

1. **You join a secure video link** at your appointment time from a private space with a stable internet connection.
2. **You and your clinician check in** on what has been happening since the last session and what you want to focus on today.
3. **You work together** on understanding patterns, practicing skills, processing emotions, or navigating a specific situation.
4. **You leave with a clearer next step**—even if that next step is simply noticing something differently before you meet again.

Sessions for individual therapy are commonly about 50 minutes once care is underway. An initial evaluation may be longer when a clinician needs more time to understand your history and goals. Your clinician will explain timing and format before you begin.

## Technology and privacy basics

You typically need:

- A phone, tablet, or computer with a camera and microphone
- A reliable internet connection
- Headphones if that helps you feel more private
- A reasonably quiet, private room where you will not be interrupted

Practices use HIPAA-aware telehealth tools designed for clinical care. Still, your environment matters: treat the session like an in-person appointment by protecting privacy on your end (closed door, headphones, avoiding public Wi‑Fi when you can).

If technology fails mid-session, clinicians usually have a backup plan (for example, switching devices or completing by phone when appropriate and allowed). Ask your practice what they do when a connection drops.

## Online vs. in-person therapy

Many people wonder whether virtual care “counts” the same as sitting in an office. For a large number of adults, online therapy can be just as effective for common concerns such as anxiety, depression, stress, relationship patterns, grief, and life transitions—especially when the clinician fit is strong and sessions are consistent.

In-person care can still be a better fit for some people or situations. TrueBridge is primarily telehealth-first statewide, with select in-person availability when scheduling allows. If you are unsure which format is right, say so when you reach out; we can help you think it through.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to live in a specific North Carolina city?

No. Statewide telehealth means you can usually work with a TrueBridge clinician while you are located anywhere in North Carolina, as long as licensing and practice rules are met for your situation.

### Is online therapy confidential?

Clinicians follow the same professional confidentiality standards as in-person care, with the usual legal exceptions (such as safety-related disclosures). You also play a role by joining from a private space.

### What if I am in crisis?

Online therapy and this website are not emergency services. If you are in immediate danger or a mental health crisis, call or text 988, call 911, or go to the nearest emergency room.
